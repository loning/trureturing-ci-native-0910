# OEIS A388986

## Provenance and preregistration

Skill context: `lean4`. One Codex implementation worker in the caller's
`consensus-rnd:sshx` implementation stage performs the source reading,
numerical checks and Lean work. No independent review or multi-model
consensus is claimed by this worker. Baseline:
`e7e63c615460da48effcb4a76f96a3a6730af75d`; branch
`lane/math/a388986-euler-form`.

The caller's target is the exact A228058 inclusion question. For natural
p,a,r with p prime, p % 4 = 1, r odd, r > 1 and gcd(p,r)=1, put
N=p^(4*a+1)*r^2. Define U(N) as the sum of divisors d of N satisfying
gcd(d,N/d)=1, and S(N) as the sum of squarefree divisors of N. Prove
U(N)+S(N)<2*N. All normalized calculations take place in the rationals;
N is positive under the hypotheses. There is no sequence-index shift.

Before any Lean implementation, the proposed escape witness is the triage
finite-product bound: for a finite set F of integers q >= b > 1,
product (1+1/q^2) <= b/(b-1). The live route splits the prime support of r
into: no 3; only 3; 3 and another prime. The source divisor sums must be
connected to their actual prime-power products. This is a first-tier small
OEIS question, conditional on the literature and bind-only checks below.

## Independent numerical evidence

`attempt-1/numeric.py` uses smallest-prime-factor factorization and an
independent divisor sieve: for each d dividing N it separately tests
`gcd(d,N/d)=1` and squarefreeness of d. All 1,000,000 positive N through
10^6 agree with both prime-product formulas (zero mismatches). Of these,
12,941 are Euler-form instances: no 3 in r: 5,905; only 3: 6,104; 3 and
another prime: 932. All satisfy both proposed class bounds and U+S<2N.
Maximum observed (U+S)/N is 28/15, attained at N=45.

Positive nonempty witness: p=5,a=0,r=3,N=45,U=60,S=24,2N=90.
Negative control: p=5,a=0,r=1,N=5,U=6,S=6,2N=10. Only r>1 fails, and the
conclusion fails. Three-class witnesses are respectively N=245 (300+48<490),
45 (60+24<90), and 2205 (3000+192<4410).
Browne's support interpretation first differs at 240: U=408,S=72,2N=480;
rad(240)=30 divides 210<240, whose support includes the additional prime 7.
The sibling interpretation is not the implementation target.

## Binding and literature checks

The scratch bind-only probe unfolds the actual two sums, tries direct
`exact?`, simplification, and omega. It does not close the inequality;
omega leaves both filtered sums unconstrained. One exploratory #check used
an unavailable lemma name; that diagnostic is separate from the failed
main-goal closure, and is not evidence of mathematical impossibility.
The two Mathlib sigma declarations cited by triage were #checked at the pin:
they sum all divisor powers. `Nat.sum_divisors_filter_squarefree` supplies
the powerset parametrization for S. Source searches in D5 and pinned
NumberTheory locate no unitary-divisor inequality theorem. D5's existing
PrimeGaps/EulerProducts has weighted sieve-coefficient estimates, with
different functions and hypotheses. Third-party GitHub code search for
`"unitary" "divisors" language:lean` returns algebraic-unitary definitions
and unrelated number-theory files; no exact target was found in that scope.
This is not a proof of bind-only impossibility or search exhaustion.

Supplement beyond the triage seat's 15 direct A entries: read Finch's full
nine-page *Unitarism and Infinitarism*, cached at
https://oeis.org/A007947/a007947.pdf. Section 0.1 proves the unitary and
squarefree divisor parametrizations; section 0.2 concerns asymptotic means
and reciprocal sums. It does not prove the Euler-form sum inequality.
Read all six pages of Tim Trudgian's *The sum of the unitary divisor
function*, Publications de l'Institut Mathématique 97(111) (2015), 175–180,
https://doi.org/10.2298/PIM140617001T, from the DOI page's linked full text.
Theorem 1.1 bounds U(N) by 1.3007*N*log(log N) for N>=570571; the later
results concern exponential divisors. None states the present joint bound.
The known prime-product formulas in A034448/A048250 are published facts;
the target inclusion in A388986 remains a question in the source.
